* # Bear-Buddy Assembly Guide

  *Your programmable DIY desktop companion*

  <img src="assets\0.BearBuddy.png" style="zoom:50%;" />

  ## Meet Bear-Buddy

  Bear-Buddy is a little desktop companion you can build, program, and make your own.

  Powered by an ESP32-C3 SuperMini, Bear-Buddy brings together a 5 × 5 RGB NeoPixel LED matrix, two tactile switches, an onboard LED, and an infrared (IR) sensor. There's plenty to explore, from colourful animations and interactive buttons to experimenting with infrared signals.

  And the best part? You can bring Bear-Buddy to life using **MicroBlocks** block coding or **CircuitPython**.

  But first, let's put it together.

  

  ## Before You Begin

  This guide assumes you've already finished soldering all the electronic components onto the Bear-Buddy controller board.

  **Haven't soldered your board yet?**

  No worries! Follow our [Bear-Buddy Electronics Soldering Guide](https://tomatocube.com/file_dir/workshop_BearBuddy/Files_v1/Misc/AssemblyGuide/) to complete the electronics first, then come back here when you're ready.

  Once your board is fully soldered, there are just three parts to the assembly:

  1. **The Display Panel** - Putting together Bear-Buddy's hands and LED display frame.
  2. **The Acrylic Backing** - Attaching the little tail to the transparent back cover.
  3. **Putting It All Together** - Fitting the electronics, display panel, and backing into the body.

  Let's get started.

  

  # Part A - The Display Panel

  We'll start with the display panel. This is the part Bear-Buddy holds in front of its belly, with two hands that also act as buttons.

  ## What You'll Need

  - 2 × Tactile switch covers
  - 2 × 3D-printed Bear-Buddy hands
  - 1 × 3D-printed display panel with a pre-installed 5 × 5 LED frame

  <img src="assets\1.Parts.png" style="zoom:67%;" />

  

  ## 1. Fit the Switch Covers

  Insert a tactile switch cover into each hand. Push them in until they sit snugly inside.

  These little covers will connect Bear-Buddy's hands to the buttons on the electronics board later.

  <img src="assets\1.FittingSwitch.png" style="zoom:80%;" />

  

  ## 2. Attach the Hands

  Slide each hand through its opening on the display panel. Push it all the way in until you hear a click.

  The click tells you the hand has snapped into place.

  **Pay attention!** Both hands are identical and can be fitted on either side. Just make sure they're facing the correct way before snapping them into place.

  <img src="assets\1.AttachHands.png" style="zoom:80%;" />

  

  ## 3. Align the Hands

  Adjust the hands so they follow the shape of the display panel.

  Both hands should sit neatly against the panel, just like Bear-Buddy is holding it.

  <img src="assets\1.AssembledParts.png" style="zoom:80%;" />

  That's the display panel ready. Put it aside for now - we'll come back to it in Part C.

  

  

  # Part B - The Acrylic Backing

  Next is the acrylic backing. This closes up the back of Bear-Buddy and holds its little tail in place.

  ## What You'll Need

  - 1 × Bear-Buddy acrylic backing
  - 1 × 3D-printed tail
  - 1 × Tail-shaped felt pad
  - 1 × M2 nylon washer
  - 1 × M2 × 4 mm metal screw

  *<img src="assets\2.Parts.png" style="zoom: 80%;" />*

  ## 1. Stick On the Felt

  Peel off the adhesive backing from the felt pad and stick it onto the tail.

  Line up the edges so the felt follows the shape of the tail.

  *<img src="assets\2.Felt.png" style="zoom: 80%;" />*

  

  ## 2. Peel Off the Protective Film

  The acrylic backing comes with a thin protective film on both sides to keep it from getting scratched.

  Peel off both films before continuing.

  

  ## 3. Fit the Tail

  Insert the tail through the opening in the acrylic backing.

  **Check the orientation.** The acrylic backing and tail have specific shapes, so make sure they're facing the right way before fitting them together.

  <img src="assets\2.FittingTail.png" style="zoom:80%;" />

  

  ## 4. Add the Washer

  Place the M2 nylon washer over the screw hole on the tail.

  The washer sits between the screw head and the tail.

  <img src="assets\2.Washer.png" style="zoom: 67%;" />

  ## 5. Secure the Tail

  Insert the M2 × 4 mm metal screw through the washer and tighten it until the tail is held securely.

  **Pay attention!** The tail should stay in place without swinging freely, but still be able to swivel out of the way when you need to plug in or unplug the USB cable.

  *<img src="assets\2.Screw.png" style="zoom:67%;" />*

  The acrylic backing is now ready. Keep it somewhere safe until the final step.

  

  # Part C - Putting It All Together

  Now for the main assembly. We'll fit the electronics into Bear-Buddy's body, attach the display panel, and close up the back.

  ## What You'll Need

  - 1 × Bear-Buddy electronics board (ESP32-C3 SuperMini, fully soldered)
  - 2 × Pieces of electrical tape
  - 1 × 3D-printed Bear-Buddy body
  - 1 × 3D-printed nose
  - 3 × M3 nylon standoffs
  - 3 × M3 × 6 mm nylon screws
  - The display panel from Part A
  - The acrylic backing from Part B

  <img src="assets\3.Parts.png" style="zoom:67%;"/>

  

  ## 1. Protect the Wire Connections

  Start with the electronics board. Place a piece of electrical tape over the soldered wire connections.

  This helps keep the wires in place and reduces strain on the solder joints while you're putting everything together.

  <img src="assets\3.Wire.png" style="zoom:80%;" />

  

  ## 2. Feed the LED Matrix Through the Body

  Hold Bear-Buddy's body with its face towards you.

  Carefully feed the NeoPixel LED matrix through the large opening in its belly. Take your time and avoid pulling on the wires.

  The LED matrix should now be sitting in front of the body, ready to fit into the display panel.

  <img src="assets\3.Neopixel.png" style="zoom: 80%;" />

  

  ## 3. Fit the Nose

  From inside the body, insert the 3D-printed nose into the opening on Bear-Buddy's face.

  Push it gently into position until it sits neatly against the face.

  *<img src="assets\3.Nose.png" style="zoom:67%;" />*

  ## 4. Secure the Electronics Board

  Position the electronics board inside the body, lining it up with the three mounting points.

  Use the three M3 nylon standoffs to secure the board in place.

  The board should sit firmly inside the body without moving around.

  <img src="assets\3.Board.png" style="zoom:80%;" />

  ## 5. Fit the LED Matrix into the Display Panel

  Take the display panel you assembled in Part A.

  Fit the NeoPixel LED matrix into the 5 × 5 frame on the back of the panel. Make sure the LEDs line up with the openings in the frame.

  <img src="assets\3.DisplayPanel.png" style="zoom: 80%;" />

  

  ## 6. Hold the LED Matrix in Place

  Apply the second piece of electrical tape to the back of the display panel to keep the LED matrix from slipping out.

  Make sure the matrix stays flat and properly aligned.

  <img src="assets\3.Tape.png" style="zoom:80%;" />

  

  ## 7. Connect the Hands to the Buttons

  Bring the display panel towards Bear-Buddy's body.

  Line up the tactile switch covers inside the hands with the two switches on the electronics board.

  Once both sides are aligned, gently press the hands onto the switches until they sit properly in place.

  **Be gentle here.** The switches are small and can be damaged if the hands are pushed in at an angle. If something doesn't fit, check the alignment rather than forcing it.

  <img src="assets\3.Hands.png" style="zoom:67%;" />

  

  ## 8. Close Up the Back

  Take the acrylic backing you assembled in Part B and place it against the back of Bear-Buddy.

  Line up the three mounting holes with the nylon standoffs inside the body.

  Insert the three M3 × 6 mm nylon screws and tighten them gently to hold the backing in place.

  Don't overtighten the screws. Once the backing is secure, you're done.

  <img src="assets\3.Backing.png" style="zoom:67%;" />

  

  # Your Bear-Buddy Is Ready!

  That's it. Your Bear-Buddy is all put together.

  Now comes the fun part - bringing it to life!

  With its programmable 5 × 5 RGB LED matrix, interactive buttons, onboard LED, and infrared sensor, there's plenty you can make Bear-Buddy do.

  You can program it with **MicroBlocks** if you prefer block coding, or **CircuitPython** if you're ready to explore Python programming.
